Custom-grow microgreens means a professional grower produces specific varieties (or a locked mix) to your kitchen’s brief — timing, volume, and plate job — instead of only pulling from a standing catalog. Use it when a tasting menu, brand signature, or seasonal story needs a finish you can repeat every service. ## Custom grow vs standing catalog (honest)

Most kitchens should win on the catalog first. Custom is for when the plate’s identity depends on it.

| Situation | Standing catalog (40+) | Custom grow |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------|
| Need it this week | Usually yes | Only if already in production / agreed lead time |
| Signature / exclusive finish | Limited | Designed for this |
| Cost predictability | Easier | Brief + volume dependent — confirm with sales |
| R&D / tasting-menu trials | Sample from list first | Lock after the plate works |
| Buyer risk | Lower | Higher if the brief is vague |

**Quotable:** Brief the plate job, not the vibe.

Cost per usable portion matters more than the sticker on the case. A cheap tray that wilts before Friday service is not cheaper. Custom programs earn their keep when a named finish — say a purple-mustard heat line or a shiso-leaning aroma — shows up identical at lunch and late Friday private dining. Plating two hundred covers is logistics: receiving days, walk-in FIFO, tweezers staged, not a one-off Instagram plate.

## What to put in a grow brief (chef + buyer)

Write the brief like mise — precise, not poetic.

- **Plate job:** aroma / color / height / crunch / heat sensitivity under lamps
- **Station:** garde manger / pass / pastry / bar
- **Target flavor family:** e.g. shiso-leaning, sunflower crunch, peppery nasturtium, purple-mustard bite
- **Volume per week** plus peak event or banquet weeks
- **Receiving days** and walk-in constraints
- **Whether edible flowers ship with micros** — blooms as food on the same card, not an afterthought garnish kit
- **Exclusivity needs:** ask honestly; don’t assume exclusivity Fuel hasn’t confirmed in writing

**Sample before you switch** from your current supplier. Taste the catalog under real pass conditions before you lock a custom SKU or rip out an incumbent PO. Ask culinary sales about current variety lists, [sample box](https://culinary.fuelfoods.store/samplebox) options, and [pairing-guide](https://culinary.fuelfoods.store/pairing-guide) materials for R&D — start from what already grows well, then custom-lock what the menu requires.

## Timeline reality (practical SOP)

Label: **no primary cite — practical SOP.**

Do not invent per-crop day counts without an ops table. Instead:

1. Agree lead time **in writing** before the menu goes to print.
2. Build buffer ahead of tasting-menu locks and PR shoots.
3. Stage R&D from the standing **40+** list when a catalog variety already does the plate job.
4. Lock custom only after the finish works under real service conditions — 5 p.m. rush and late Friday alike.

Custom grow is rarely the fastest path to “something unique next Tuesday.” It’s the path to a repeatable, named finish once the plate is proven.

## Where custom grow fails

- Vague “make us something unique” with no plate job, height, or flavor family
- Next-day exclusivity on a never-grown SKU
- Treating custom as a substitute for walk-in FIFO and plating discipline — see [plating microgreens for chefs](/blog/plating-microgreens-for-chefs)
- Wellness or disease stories on the menu card — chefs sell taste and craft, not cures
- Ordering custom volume without receiving SOP — waste kills cost-per-cover math

Documentary kitchen tone, not brochure tone: the ticket printer doesn’t pause for a brand story. The green either works under heat and fingers, or it doesn’t.

## Banquet / hotel / private dining (short)

Volume spikes and pre-portion needs change the brief. A locked custom mix can stabilize a card across ballrooms when every station needs the same finish. You still need receiving windows, cold holding, and portion discipline — custom grow doesn’t erase BOH reality. Deep banquet math is a future spoke; start the conversation with weekly base volume plus peak event weeks in the email.
